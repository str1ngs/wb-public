How to Choose a Wall Charger

Wall chargers all look the same on a spec sheet until you actually try to charge two things at once. Here's what the numbers on the box actually mean.

Total wattage isn't per-port wattage

A charger listed as "300W" doesn't mean every port delivers 300W — that's the combined maximum across all ports in use simultaneously, and it's rarely split evenly. Manufacturers publish a per-port breakdown for exactly this reason: the Ugreen Nexode 300W, for instance, delivers 140W on its first port, 100W on its second, and 60W on its third — not 100W across all three. Always check the per-port table, not just the headline number, especially if you plan to charge a laptop.

GaN vs. silicon: why it matters

Gallium nitride (GaN) chargers run cooler and pack more wattage into a smaller case than older silicon-based chargers, which is why a 250–300W multi-port charger today is roughly the size of what a single 65W charger was a few years ago. Nearly every charger worth buying in 2026 uses GaN — it's not a premium feature anymore, it's the baseline.

How many ports do you actually need?

  • 1–2 ports: fine for a single person's phone + laptop at a nightstand or travel bag
  • 4–5 ports: a genuine desk replacement for laptop, phone, tablet, and earbuds case, like the Anker Prime 250W
  • 5+ ports with high per-port wattage: only worth it for shared desks or households charging multiple laptops simultaneously

Do you need the display and app?

Chargers with an LCD display and companion app (like the Anker Prime Charger) let you see and adjust real-time wattage per port, which is genuinely useful for troubleshooting slow charging or setting limits for battery-sensitive devices. It's a real feature, not just a gimmick — but chargers without one, like the Ugreen Nexode 300W, are usually cheaper for similar or higher raw output if you don't need the visibility.

Rule of thumb: match the charger to your heaviest single device first (a laptop needing 100–140W dictates the charger you need far more than how many phones you own), then make sure remaining ports cover everything else you'll realistically plug in at once.
