The F3800 is Anker's answer to "what if a power station could replace a portable generator entirely." At 3,840Wh and 6,000W of dual-voltage output, it's less a camping accessory and more a wheeled appliance built to sit in a garage and take over the moment the grid drops.

Who it's for

The F3800 is built for homeowners in outage-prone areas who want to power a fridge, well pump, and a handful of 240V circuits without running a gas generator in the driveway. It also doubles as a direct EV or RV charger through its NEMA 14-50 and L14-30 ports.

Strengths

  • True 120V/240V split-phase output — no step-up converter needed
  • Expandable from 3.84kWh to 53.8kWh across two units and 12 battery packs
  • 2,400W solar input across dual MPPT channels for sub-2-hour solar recharge
  • AC coupling support — one of few portable units that ties into existing home solar

Trade-offs

  • Premium price relative to single-unit 2–3kWh competitors
  • Base capacity (3.84kWh) alone covers about a day of essential loads — full potential requires expansion batteries, which add significant cost
  • Size and weight make it a stationary/wheeled unit, not something you carry to a campsite

Charging and expansion

Anker rates the solar input at up to 2,400W across two 60V MPPT channels, enough for a full recharge in under two hours of strong sun. AC wall charging tops out around 1,800W. Where the F3800 stands apart is expansion: each BP3800 battery pack adds another 3.84kWh, with support for up to six packs on a single unit (26.9kWh total), or two F3800 units plus twelve packs for a 53.8kWh whole-home system.

Frequently asked questions

How long will the Anker SOLIX F3800 power a house?

On its own 3,840Wh charge, it typically covers essential loads (fridge, lights, WiFi, some outlets) for about a day.

Does the Anker SOLIX F3800 support 240V appliances?

Yes. It outputs both 120V and 240V split-phase power through its built-in NEMA L14-30 and NEMA 14-50 ports, so it can run dryers, well pumps, or EV chargers without a separate step-up converter.

Can the F3800 charge from solar panels?

Yes, it accepts up to 2,400W of solar input across two MPPT channels, which Anker states can fully recharge the unit in under two hours of strong sunlight.
